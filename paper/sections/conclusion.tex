\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a genetic algorithm for joint optimisation of garment pattern
layout, seam-aware texture alignment, and garment fit preservation.
By optimising seam positions directly on the 3D body mesh, our method
grounds all pattern modifications in body geometry, making fit deviation
an explicit and measurable objective rather than an implicit side effect of
2D patch deformation.
A global phase alignment stage and a discrete texture phase offset per patch
allow the algorithm to exploit the full periodic symmetry of the fabric,
which we model across a set of wallpaper groups including those with
glide-reflection symmetry.
Qualitative and quantitative comparisons against two baselines on TODO
garment types demonstrate that the genetic algorithm finds solutions that
balance fabric efficiency, texture alignment, and fit preservation in ways
that greedy strategies cannot achieve.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The nesting engine uses a bottom-left heuristic, which is simple and
sufficient for our purposes but does not guarantee optimal fabric utilisation
for the nesting sub-problem in isolation.
A practical limitation of the current pipeline is that a small fraction of
individuals in the population fail during geometry processing: geodesic path
computation or LSCM parameterization can fail for certain seam configurations,
causing those individuals to receive a penalty fitness and be excluded from
selection.
Reducing this failure rate through more robust geometry processing would
improve the effective population size and convergence behaviour.
