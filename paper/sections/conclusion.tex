\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a genetic algorithm for joint optimisation of garment pattern
layout, seam-aware texture alignment, and garment fit preservation.
By optimising seam positions directly on the 3D body mesh, our method
grounds all pattern modifications in body geometry, making fit deviation
an explicit and measurable objective.
A global phase alignment stage and discrete texture phase offsets per patch
exploit the full periodic symmetry of the fabric, modelled across eight
wallpaper groups including those with glide-reflection symmetry.
For complex garments where the combinatorial phase space makes exhaustive
search intractable, the GA is the only method that can jointly explore seam
positions and phase assignments.
Experiments on two garment types with up to 1{,}100 pattern pieces
demonstrate that the GA consistently improves texture alignment over all
baselines without sacrificing fabric efficiency.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
The nesting engine uses a bottom-left heuristic; substituting an
NFP-based solver~\cite{MartinezSykoraACRO22} could improve fabric
utilisation without affecting the other contributions.
A small fraction of individuals fail during geometry processing (geodesic
or LSCM failures), receiving penalty fitness; more robust geometry
processing would improve effective population size.
The discrete Voronoi structure of the $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ landscape
(Section~\ref{sec:ea}) causes many neutral mutations; adaptive step-size
control or landscape-aware operators could accelerate convergence.
Finally, extending the framework to multiple body shapes and sizes within
a single fabric roll is a natural next step toward industrial deployment.
