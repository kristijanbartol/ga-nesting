\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Garments made from patterned fabric require periodic motifs, stripes,
checks, herringbone, to continue seamlessly across seam lines.
Achieving this alignment while minimising fabric waste involves two coupled
subproblems: \emph{nesting} (arranging 2D pattern pieces compactly on the
fabric roll) and \emph{texture alignment} (matching pattern phase at seams).
Despite their practical coupling, these problems have only been studied in
isolation.
Classical nesting methods~\cite{BennellO08} and current industry practice
place panels purely for compactness, ignoring texture continuity entirely.

Wolff et al.~\cite{WolffS19} address texture alignment by directly
deforming 2D patch shapes to minimise phase mismatch along seams.
However, their formulation assumes unbounded fabric and ignores material
efficiency.
More fundamentally, deforming flat patches changes the garment's fit
implicitly: there is no mechanism to measure or bound how much the modified
pattern deviates from the original design.

Our key insight is that seam placement is more naturally controlled on the
3D body surface than by deforming flat patterns.
Repositioning a seam line on the body mesh changes the 2D pattern only as a
consequence of re-flattening, keeping every modification grounded in body
geometry.
This makes fit deviation an explicit, measurable quantity that can be
directly penalised during optimisation.
We formalise this into a joint optimisation over fabric efficiency,
seam-aware texture alignment, and fit preservation: three objectives that
have not previously been considered together.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate garment pattern layout as a joint optimisation over
          seam positions, fabric efficiency, and texture alignment under
          realistic fabric roll constraints, the first method to address
          all three objectives simultaneously.

    \item We propose a 3D-grounded seam optimisation in which fit deviation
          is an explicit, measurable quantity used as a penalty guard
          against garment shrinkage, in contrast to prior 2D deformation
          approaches where fit change is implicit and unmeasured.

    \item We model texture periodicity via eight wallpaper symmetry groups,
          including patterns with glide-reflection symmetry such as
          herringbone, chevron, and brick bond.

    \item We evaluate on three garment types ($M \in \{2, 4, 11\}$) with
          layouts of up to 2{,}200 pattern pieces across 10 independent
          seeds, demonstrating consistent improvement over all baselines
          including random search at matched evaluation budget.
\end{enumerate}
