\section{Experiments}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

We compare against three baselines, all of which fix seam positions at the
midpoints of their respective landmark quads ($\boldsymbol{\delta} = 0.5$).
All baselines include Stage~2 global phase alignment (Section~\ref{sec:stage2})
for a fair comparison.

\paragraph{B0: Pure greedy nesting.}
All phase offsets are set to zero ($\boldsymbol{\kappa} = \mathbf{0}$),
patches are placed in descending order of area using the bottom-left heuristic,
and no grain rotation is applied.
This represents the classical industry approach: optimising fabric efficiency
with no texture awareness.

\paragraph{B1: Greedy seam-order nesting with greedy phase selection.}
Patches are placed in breadth-first order over the seam adjacency graph,
starting from the largest patch in each connected component.
When a patch is placed, its phase offset $\kappa_i$ is chosen greedily by
evaluating all $K$ candidates and selecting the one that minimises the total
weighted mismatch with already-placed seam neighbours.
Grain orientations remain fixed at $\boldsymbol{\rho} = \mathbf{0}$.
This baseline captures the best texture alignment achievable by a greedy
strategy without global optimisation or seam-position search.

\paragraph{B2: Exhaustive optimal phase selection.}
Phase offsets are assigned by exhaustive search over all $K^M$ combinations,
selecting the assignment that minimises total seam phase mismatch.
With $K=8$ and $M=4$ patches this requires $8^4 = 4096$ evaluations and
remains tractable.
B2 provides a practical upper bound on what is achievable without seam
repositioning, making it the implicit ablation for the $\boldsymbol{\delta}$
component of our method: any improvement of our GA over B2 on $f_2$ is
attributable solely to optimising seam positions.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:implementation}

The GA uses a population of 50 individuals evolved for 10 generations with
elite count 4 and tournament size 4.
Crossover is applied with probability 0.7 and mutation with probability 0.7.
Per-variable mutation rates are: $p_\kappa = 0.35$ (kappa flip),
$p_\pi = 0.3$ (permutation swap), $p_h = 0.25$ (heuristic resample).
Delta is mutated by Gaussian perturbation with $\sigma_\delta = 0.20$,
clamped to the range $[0.3, 0.7]$ to avoid geometrically degenerate mesh
regions near landmark quad boundaries.
We use $K=8$ phase bins per period.

The fabric roll width is $W = 1{,}500\,\text{mm}$ with a texture period of
$50 \times 50\,\text{mm}$.
Fitness weights are $w_1 = 1$, $w_2 = 1$, $w_4 = 10$.
All experiments run $N \in \{5, 10, 25, 50, 100\}$ bodies per roll and are
repeated with 20 independent random seeds; we report mean $\pm$ standard
deviation.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Table~\ref{tab:results_lower} reports results for the pants garment across
all body counts.
The GA consistently reduces seam phase mismatch $f_2$ relative to all
baselines, including B2 which has exhaustive optimal phase offsets.
Since B2 represents the best achievable $f_2$ with fixed $\boldsymbol{\delta}=0.5$,
any improvement over B2 is attributable to the optimisation of seam positions.
Fabric efficiency $f_1$ is comparable to B1 across all body counts, confirming
that improved texture alignment does not come at the cost of material waste.
The fit guard term $f_4 = 0$ for all GA runs, confirming that the algorithm
does not exploit seam repositioning to shrink the garment.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}

\paragraph{Seam position optimisation ($\boldsymbol{\delta}$).}
B2 provides the ablation for the seam position component: it uses the same
Stage~2 alignment but fixes $\boldsymbol{\delta} = 0.5$ and searches
exhaustively for the best phase offsets.
Our GA improves over B2 by approximately 13\% on $f_2$,
demonstrating that repositioning seams on the body mesh contributes
meaningfully to texture alignment beyond what is achievable by phase offset
optimisation alone.
