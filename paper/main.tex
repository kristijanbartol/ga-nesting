\begin{abstract}
Placing 2D garment panels on a fabric roll efficiently is a well-studied
combinatorial optimisation problem, yet existing methods ignore whether
periodic patterns such as stripes or checks align across the seams of the
assembled garment.
Prior work on texture alignment assumes an unbounded fabric supply and
operates by directly deforming 2D patch shapes, leaving fit changes implicit
and unmeasured.
We propose a joint optimisation framework that simultaneously minimises
fabric consumption, seam-wise texture misalignment, and garment fit
deviation.
Seam positions are optimised directly on the 3D body mesh, grounding fit
changes in body geometry so they can be explicitly measured and bounded.
The periodic structure of the fabric is modelled via wallpaper symmetry
groups, including patterns with glide-reflection symmetry such as
herringbone and chevron.
The problem is solved with a genetic algorithm using variable-specific
operators for its mixed genotype of continuous seam parameters, discrete
phase offsets, and grain orientations.
For complex garments, the combinatorial phase space ($K^M > 10^9$) makes
exhaustive search intractable, and the GA is the only method that can
jointly explore seam positions and phase assignments.
Experiments on two garment types with layouts of up to 1{,}100 pattern
pieces and 20 independent seeds show that the GA consistently improves
texture alignment over all baselines without sacrificing fabric efficiency.
\keywords{garment pattern nesting \and texture alignment \and
evolutionary algorithm \and wallpaper groups \and seam optimisation}
\end{abstract}
