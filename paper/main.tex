\begin{abstract}
When assembling a garment from patterned fabric, periodic motifs such as
stripes or checks should continue seamlessly across seam lines, yet
the two subproblems involved, nesting (minimising fabric waste) and texture
alignment (matching pattern phase at seams), have only been studied in
isolation.
Prior texture-alignment work assumes an unbounded fabric supply and deforms
2D patch shapes directly, leaving garment fit changes implicit and
unmeasured.
We propose the first joint optimisation of fabric efficiency, seam-wise
texture alignment, and fit preservation.
Our key idea is to optimise seam positions on the 3D body mesh rather than
deforming flat patterns: this grounds every modification in body geometry,
making fit deviation an explicit, measurable objective.
Texture periodicity is modelled via wallpaper symmetry groups, covering
eight pattern families including herringbone, chevron, and brick bond.
We solve the resulting mixed search space (continuous seam parameters,
discrete phase offsets, grain orientations; up to $K^M > 10^9$
phase configurations) with a mixed-integer evolution strategy using
variable-specific operators.
Experiments on three garment types with up to 2{,}200 pattern pieces and
10 independent seeds show consistent improvement in texture alignment over
all baselines including random search at matched evaluation budget.
\keywords{garment pattern nesting \and texture alignment \and
evolutionary algorithm \and wallpaper groups \and seam optimisation}
\end{abstract}
